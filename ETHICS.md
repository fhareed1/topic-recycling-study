# Ethics and data handling

## What was collected

- **Sale catalogues.** Only the XML sitemaps that each site publishes for crawlers. Titles are
  reconstructed from URL slugs. No product page, preview, paid document or account area was
  requested, and nothing was purchased.
- **University repository.** Dublin Core metadata (title, type, date, collection) from the
  University of Ibadan repository over OAI-PMH, the interface repositories provide for
  harvesting. Author names are present in that metadata but are not stored by the harvester.

## Politeness

- robots.txt was read for every site on 2026-09-14 before harvesting. All fetched paths are allowed.
- premiumresearchers.com was excluded because its robots.txt disallows AI crawlers, and this
  study was built with AI assistance.
- A fixed delay of 2 to 3 seconds precedes every request, with exponential backoff on errors.
  The whole harvest is a few hundred requests.
- The User-Agent names the study and links to this repository.
- TLS certificates are verified against the operating system trust store. repository.ui.edu.ng
  presents a chain that Python's bundled CA list rejects as expired; the OS store validates it.

## What is and is not published

- Published: code, aggregate results, figures, and the labelled pair sample needed to check precision.
- Not published: the raw sitemap files and the full harvested title lists (`data/` is gitignored).
  Anyone can regenerate them with `harvest/`. This avoids redistributing whole commercial catalogues.
- No individual student, author or supervisor is named or identified anywhere in the results.

## Interpretation limits

- A title match shows topic reuse. It does not show that any student bought or submitted a
  purchased project, and it cannot show direction: a sale site may have copied a real thesis title.
- Sitemap `lastmod` dates are regenerated by the sites, so they cannot date when a title was first listed.
- One repository (University of Ibadan) is not all Nigerian universities, and its holdings are
  mostly postgraduate work, while the catalogues are mostly undergraduate topics.
